# Validating LLM/VLM-simulated online shoppers against real human data: annotated literature (2022–2026)

Scope note. These notes were compiled on 2026-10-25. Every arXiv entry below was checked against the arXiv API (title, authors, date, abstract) and every journal entry against the Crossref DOI record, unless it is marked **[unverified]**. Details marked "(from full text)" were read from the paper's arXiv HTML version. Figures marked **[not re-verified]** come from prior knowledge and were not confirmed in this session; treat them as leads, not citations. The session's web-search budget ran out partway through, so a few marketing-journal results could only be checked down to their bibliographic record (see Gaps).

Entry format: **Citation**, then URL · *Simulated*: what was simulated · *Human data*: what it was compared with · *Metrics*: how the comparison was measured · *Results*: key numbers.

## 1. Online-shopping agents compared with real humans (newest first)

### Takeaway
Since 2025 there is a fast-growing line of work, much of it from one Northeastern/Amazon cluster (Lu, Wang, Gesi, D. Wang and coauthors), that scores LLM shoppers against real clickstreams. Prompt-only LLMs reproduce a specific human's next web action only about 8–22% of the time, and they are systematically too goal-directed: they search less, buy more and seldom leave. Fine-tuning or RL on real sessions roughly doubles exact-match accuracy (to about 28–40%). A/B-level evidence is still thin. There is one published web A/B case where the direction matched (AgentA/B) and one recommender study that reports a correlation with 55 real A/B tests (SimUSER).

### Cited Findings
- **Lee, M. (2026). TwinStore: A Synthetic Consumer Platform Using Vision–Language Models. arXiv:2610.30909.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30909 · *Simulated*: VLM shoppers in physical stores who look at rendered shelves and produce a receipt. · *Human data*: "eleven public datasets" (held-out). · *Metrics*: category-mix divergence measured against households' own natural drift; price elasticities checked against field ranges; agreement across three decision models. · *Results*: simulated receipts have a category-mix divergence of 0.017–0.018, against a real household drift of 0.012, and elasticities fall "inside the range measured in the field." This appears to be the platform's own physical-retail preprint. Its validation template (simulation error benchmarked against natural human variation) carries over directly to the online module. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30909)
- **Wadi, D. & Ma, Y. (2026). Does Rank Still Matter? Position Bias When AI Agents Shop on Our Behalf. arXiv:2608.22697.** · *Simulated*: LLM agents doing hotel search (Manhattan) on a 100-listing page with an `inspect` tool (the analogue of a click) and a `submit_choice` tool. Order was fully randomized per session. 7,000 sessions across five proprietary LLMs × 2–4 reasoning-effort levels, plus 2,000 sessions on open-weight models. · *Human data*: Expedia field-experiment data from Ursu (2018): 166,036 searches and 1,220,917 impressions. · *Metrics*: per-rank inspection and choice penalties (regression slopes), search depth, share of single-inspection sessions, outside-option (no booking) rate, and a quadratic "lost-in-the-middle" test. · *Results*: the per-rank inspection penalty is −0.0019 for humans and at most −0.0008 for LLMs. Mean inspections per session are 1.12 for humans and 1.63–5.83 for LLMs. 93% of human sessions have a single inspection, against 0.2–37.4% for LLMs. Humans who clicked did not book in 34% of sessions, while no LLM ever chose the outside option. LLMs show a middle-rank penalty (minimum at ranks 62–81) in 12 of 14 conditions, and higher reasoning effort reduced it. (From full text.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22697)
- **Tang, J., Mi, Y., Yu, J., … Chen, X. (2026). Towards Faithful Simulation of Human Shopping Behavior. arXiv:2608.20707.** · *Simulated*: RecVerse, a **GUI-grounded agent that perceives screenshots** and produces multi-turn e-commerce sessions using hierarchical memory and a trajectory-level RL objective. · *Human data*: real browsing sessions. The authors release **USB (User Simulation Benchmark)**, an interactive e-commerce GUI trajectory dataset. · *Metrics*: alignment of macro-level action-type distributions and micro-level shopping intent with real users. · *Results*: the abstract claims RecVerse "significantly outperforms existing baselines". It also notes that step-supervised simulators show "over-exploration or excessive passivity." Specific numbers were not extracted. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20707)
- **Chen, L. (2026). Simulated Customers Never Walk Away: Decision Fidelity of LLM User Simulators Measured Against Real Purchase Outcomes. arXiv:2606.20708.** · *Simulated*: LLM customers in sales conversations. · *Human data*: 2,790 production conversations with real customers, 793 of them with verified payment outcomes. · *Metrics*: "decision fidelity", using a teacher-forced probe; depth bias and Cohen's d. · *Results*: the "disengagement deficit". Simulators reproduce eventual buyers well (depth bias +0.09) but push non-buyers toward purchase (+0.40, d = 0.38, p < .001). Expressed resistance falls from 25.1% to 13.5%, and deliberation rises from 21.9% to 40.1%. Telling the simulator it may disengage cuts marginal bias five-fold, yet d stays at 0.34. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20708)
- **Zanjani Foumani, Z., Castelo, A., Xie, S., Chaiwachirasak, T., Li, H., Wang, L. (2026). SimPersona: Learning Discrete Buyer Personas from Raw Clickstreams for Grounded E-Commerce Agents. arXiv:2605.14205.** · *Simulated*: LLM web agents on **live storefronts**, conditioned on persona tokens learned by a VQ-VAE from clickstreams. · *Human data*: 8.37M buyers across 42 held-out live storefronts. · *Metrics*: conversion-rate alignment with real buyers. · *Results*: **78% conversion-rate alignment**. The paper states that prompt-based personas make agents "collapse to a single 'average buyer' policy." It releases an open-source pipeline that turns raw event logs into agent-training traces. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14205)
- **Zhong, R., McDonald, D. W., Hsieh, G. (2025). Synthetic Cognitive Walkthrough: Aligning LLM Performance with Human Cognitive Walkthrough. arXiv:2512.03568.** · *Simulated*: GPT-4 and Gemini-2.5-pro doing UI walkthroughs. · *Human data*: human participants doing the same cognitive walkthrough. · *Results*: LLMs had **higher task completion and more optimal navigation paths** than humans and found fewer failure points. Extra prompting let them predict the failure points humans identified. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03568)
- **Bansal, G., Hua, W., Huang, Z., … Hofman, J. M., et al. (2025). Magentic Marketplace: An Open-Source Environment for Studying Agentic Markets. arXiv:2510.25779 (Microsoft).** · *Simulated*: two-sided markets of consumer Assistant agents and business Service agents. · *Human data*: none (welfare is compared against an optimum). · *Results*: all models show a severe **first-proposal bias**, which gives speed a 10–30× advantage over quality. Welfare degrades with market scale. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25779)
- **Wang, Z., Lu, Y., Zhang, Y., Huang, J., Wang, D. (2025). Customer-R1: Personalized Simulation of Human Behaviors via RL-based LLM Agent in Online Shopping. arXiv:2510.07230.** · *Simulated*: persona-conditioned next action and rationale (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-1M). · *Human data*: OPeRA-filtered (527 sessions, 5,856 action–observation pairs, 49 users). · *Metrics*: next-action accuracy, action-type macro-F1, fine-grained type accuracy, session-outcome weighted-F1. · *Results* (Table 4): next-action accuracy is 7.32% zero-shot, 35.14% with SFT and **39.58% with SFT+RL**. Macro-F1 is 78.50 and outcome F1 79.45. **Shuffling personas drops accuracy to 28.94%**, which is evidence that the persona carries signal. No KL or other distribution-distance metric is reported, only qualitative action-distribution plots. (From full text.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07230)
- **Allouah, A., Besbes, O., Figueroa, J. D., Kanoria, Y., Kumar, A. (2025). What Is Your AI Agent Buying? Evaluation, Biases, Model Dependence, & Emerging Implications for Agentic E-Commerce. arXiv:2508.02630.** Introduces the **ACES** sandbox. · *Simulated*: a VLM agent (Selenium) **looking at a screenshot of a mock storefront** with 8 products in a 2×4 grid and making one choice. Positions are permuted uniformly at random, and badges, prices, ratings and review counts are randomized. There is also a text-only "headless" condition. · *Human data*: **none collected**. Agent price elasticities are compared only informally with published human brand-level estimates. · *Metrics*: conditional-logit coefficients and market shares. · *Results*: strong, **model-specific position effects**. For example, Claude Sonnet 4 has a top-row coefficient of 1.224, while GPT-5.1's is −0.701. Choice is homogeneous: Claude Opus 4.5 picks Fitbit Inspire 77% of the time. "Sponsored" tags reduce choice: a 10% baseline drops to 8.0–8.9%. "Overall Pick" raises it: 10% becomes 19.9–42.6%. Implied price elasticities are about −1.6 to −2.2. Model updates reshuffle market shares. (From full text.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02630)
- **Zhang, Y., Wang, T., Gesi, J., Wang, Z., Lu, Y., et al. (2025). Shop-R1: Rewarding LLMs to Simulate Human Behavior in Online Shopping via Reinforcement Learning. ICLR 2026. arXiv:2507.17842.** · *Human data*: "Shop-CART", described as 52,137 real sessions "from a leading global e-commerce service". The paper does not say whether it is public. OPeRA appears in the appendix. · *Metrics*: exact action accuracy, action-type accuracy and F1. · *Results* (Qwen-2.5-3B): exact accuracy is 0.32% zero-shot, 16.76% with SFT and **27.72% with Shop-R1**. Action-type accuracy reaches 36.40%. (From full text.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17842)
- **Wang, Z., Lu, Y., Li, W., Amini, A., Sun, B., Bart, Y., et al. (2025). OPeRA: A Dataset of Observation, Persona, Rationale, and Action for Evaluating LLMs on Human Online Shopping Behavior Simulation. ACL 2026 (main). arXiv:2506.05606.** · *Human data*: real Amazon.com sessions collected over 4 weeks with a browser plugin and a questionnaire. OPeRA-full has **51 users, 692 sessions, 28,904 action–observation pairs and 604 just-in-time self-reported rationales**. OPeRA-filtered has 527 sessions and 5,856 pairs. Personas include age, gender, education, income, shopping habits, an 8-item Consumer Styles Inventory, Big Five and MBTI. · *Metrics*: next-action exact match, action-type weighted and macro F1, click-type F1, session-outcome accuracy and F1. · *Results*: **GPT-4.1 reaches 21.51% next-action accuracy**, Claude-3.7 10.75%, DeepSeek-R1 14.75% and Llama-3.3 8.31%. Removing the persona barely changes exact accuracy (GPT-4.1 rises to 22.06%) but lowers macro-F1. The authors conclude that models do not integrate persona well. The participant count is inconsistent in the text (84 vs "8484"); 51 contributing users is the reliable figure. (From full text.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05606)
- **Lu, Y., Hsu, T.-Y., Gu, H., Cui, L., Xie, Y., Headden, W., Yao, B., Veeragouni, A., et al. (2025). AgentA/B: Automated and Scalable Web A/B Testing with Interactive LLM Agents. arXiv:2504.09723.** · *Simulated*: 1,000 Claude 3.5 Sonnet agents (UXAgent framework) on **live Amazon.com**, 500 per arm. The treatment pruned filter options with less than 80% similarity to the query. Personas were sampled from 100k generated personas matched to online-shopper demographics. · *Human data*: a parallel real A/B test on the same feature. The paper gives the size inconsistently: "2M participants" in the text, and "N=1M" for the human control column. · *Metrics*: per-session action counts, purchases, spend, and a χ² test of the treatment effect. · *Results*: **humans 15.96 actions/session vs agents 6.05; searches 6.40 vs 1.42; product clicks 6.96 vs 1.87; purchase 0.62 vs 0.81; filter clicks 0.33 vs 0.58.** The treatment raised agent purchases (414 vs 403; χ²(1) = 5.51, p = 0.03), and the paper says the direction matched the human experiment. The human treatment-arm numbers are **not reported**. This is a single feature on a single site. (From full text.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09723)
- **Lu, Y., Yao, B., Gu, H., Huang, J., Wang, J., Li, Y., Gesi, J., He, Q., et al. (2025). UXAgent: A System for Simulating Usability Testing of Web Design with LLM Agents. arXiv:2504.09407.** The companion paper is *UXAgent: An LLM Agent-Based Usability Testing Framework for Web Design*, arXiv:2502.12561 (described as CHI 2025 extended abstract; **venue [not re-verified]**). · *Simulated*: thousands of persona-driven agents with post-study surveys and agent interviews. · *Human data*: **none for behavior**. It was evaluated through heuristic evaluations with 5 UX researchers (2502.12561) and 16 UX researchers (2504.09407). This is the closest architectural analogue to TwinStore's exit survey and interview features. — [arXiv 2504.09407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.09407); [arXiv 2502.12561](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12561)
- **Lu, Y., Huang, J., Han, Y., Yao, B., Bei, S., Gesi, J., Xie, Y., Sang, Y., et al. (2025). Can LLM Agents Simulate Multi-Turn Human Behavior? Evidence from Real Online Customer Behavior Data. arXiv:2503.20749.** · *Human data*: **31,865 Amazon.com sessions with 230,965 actions** from consenting beta users, with PII removed. The footnote points to Hugging Face `NEU-HAI/ShopCART`. · *Action space*: click, type_and_submit, terminate. · *Metrics*: next-action exact match (averaged per session) and buy-vs-terminate F1. · *Results*: **the best prompt-only model, DeepSeek-R1, reaches 11.86%** (Claude 3.5 Sonnet v2 11.69%, Llama-3.1-70B 8.19%). Fine-tuned Qwen2.5-7B with synthesized reasoning reaches **17.26%**, with outcome F1 33.86%. On OPeRA, GPT-4.1 gets 21.28% and fine-tuned Qwen with reasoning 35.14%. In the qualitative error analysis, LLMs keep their first query, overuse filters and show "disproportionately high purchase rates". Humans average 2.82 searches per session. The authors suggest that task-completion benchmarks such as WebShop and WebArena push models toward purchase-heavy behavior. (From full text.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20749)
- **Benchmarks with human demonstrations or human baselines.** These measure task success rather than human-likeness, but reviewers will expect them to be cited:
  - **Xue, T., et al. (2025). An Illusion of Progress? Assessing the Current State of Web Agents. COLM 2025. arXiv:2504.01382.** Online-Mind2Web: 300 tasks on 136 live websites. An LLM-as-judge achieves about 85% agreement with humans. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01382)
  - **Cai, H., Li, Y., Wang, W., Zhu, F., Shen, X., Li, W., Chua, T.-S. (2025). Large Language Models Empowered Personalized Web Agents. WWW 2025. arXiv:2410.17236.** PersonalWAB benchmark: user profiles plus historical web behaviors. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17236)
  - **Jin, Y., Li, Z., Zhang, C., et al. (2024). Shopping MMLU. NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks. arXiv:2410.20745.** 57 tasks built from Amazon data, including a "user behavior alignment" skill. Used for KDD Cup 2024. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20745)
  - **Koh, J. Y., et al. (2024). VisualWebArena. ACL 2024. arXiv:2401.13649.** Visually grounded tasks, including a classifieds/shopping site. Shows the limits of text-only agents. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.13649)
  - **Peng, B., Ling, X., Chen, Z., Sun, H., Ning, X. (2024). eCeLLM / ECInstruct. ICML 2024. arXiv:2402.08831.** E-commerce instruction data. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.08831)
  - **Deng, X., et al. (2023). Mind2Web. NeurIPS 2023 (Spotlight). arXiv:2306.06070.** More than 2,000 tasks on 137 sites, with crowdsourced human action sequences. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06070)
  - **Zhou, S., et al. (2023). WebArena. arXiv:2307.13854.** Includes an e-commerce site. The best GPT-4 agent succeeded 14.41% of the time vs 78.24% for humans. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13854)
  - **Yao, S., Chen, H., Yang, J., Narasimhan, K. (2022). WebShop. NeurIPS 2022. arXiv:2207.01206.** 1.18M products, 12,087 instructions and **more than 1,600 human demonstrations**. Best model 29% success vs human experts 59%. The paper analyzes agent vs human trajectories. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01206)

### Inferences
- The strongest prior-work anchors for a TwinStore paper are Lu et al. 2025 (11.86%), OPeRA (21.5%), Customer-R1 and Shop-R1 (up to about 40% after training), AgentA/B (A/B direction) and ACES (screenshot-based choice with randomized positions). Wadi & Ma (2026) is the best template for comparing agents with *human field data* on positional behavior.
- **Exact next-action accuracy is a low-ceiling metric.** Even fine-tuned models sit at about 17–40%. A paper claiming "behaves like real users" will be read against these numbers, so it is safer to claim **distributional and aggregate fidelity** (funnel rates, session lengths, A/B direction) than individual step accuracy, unless OPeRA-style per-step evaluation is also reported.
- The consistent finding of over-purchase and too-short, too-direct sessions (AgentA/B, Lu et al., Wadi & Ma, Chen 2026, Zhong 2025) is the main threat a reviewer will raise. TwinStore's "leave" option, the patience trait and missions such as browse and compare are the right levers. The paper should show bounce, abandonment and no-purchase rates against a human benchmark (Section 2) rather than assert them.
- TwinStore's same-shopper paired A/B design closely mirrors AgentA/B and ACES randomized trials. AgentA/B did not publish the human treatment effect, which leaves room to contribute a multi-test directional-agreement study.

### Gaps
- No published study yet validates **VLM-from-screenshot** online shoppers against human clickstreams at the session level. ACES uses screenshots but has no human data. RecVerse uses screenshots, but its numbers were not extracted here.
- AgentA/B's human sample size (1M vs 2M) and purchase counts (403 vs 404) are internally inconsistent, and the human treatment-arm result is not reported.
- The Shop-CART size is 31,865 sessions in Lu et al. but 52,137 in Shop-R1. Its license on Hugging Face (`NEU-HAI/ShopCART`) is unspecified; the API returned no license field.
- Exit-survey fidelity (satisfaction, SEQ, NPS) for web-shopping agents has not been validated against humans in any paper found. UXAgent collects agent surveys but does not compare them with humans.

## 2. Human ground-truth datasets usable for validation

### Takeaway
Only a few public datasets contain **real human online-shopping trajectories with persona information**. OPeRA (CC-BY-4.0) is the main one, and ShopCART appears to be released, though without a stated license. Session-level funnel statistics can be benchmarked against older clickstream datasets (UCI Online Shoppers, Coveo SIGIR eCom 2021, Amazon-M2). Position effects can be benchmarked against the Expedia field experiment (Ursu 2018). Survey-response fidelity has its own individual-level ground truth in Twin-2K-500.

### Cited Findings
- **OPeRA** (Wang et al. 2025): Amazon.com, 51 users, 692 sessions, 28,904 action–observation pairs, 604 rationales. Includes screenshots/HTML observations and rich personas. Hugging Face `NEU-HAI/OPeRA`, **license cc-by-4.0** (HF API), not gated. — [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NEU-HAI/OPeRA); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05606)
- **ShopCART** (Lu et al. 2025): 31,865 Amazon sessions and 230,965 actions. Hugging Face `NEU-HAI/ShopCART`, public, 100K–1M rows, **no license tag**. — [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/NEU-HAI/ShopCART); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.20749)
- **USB (User Simulation Benchmark)** (Tang et al. 2026): an interactive e-commerce GUI trajectory dataset. Size and license not extracted. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20707)
- **SimPersona pipeline** (2026): open-source code that converts raw e-commerce event logs into buyer representations. The underlying merchant data (8.37M buyers) is not described as public. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14205)
- **UCI Online Shoppers Purchasing Intention Dataset** (Sakar, Polat, Katircioglu & Kastro, *Neural Computing and Applications*, 2019, doi:10.1007/s00521-018-3523-0): **12,330 sessions; 84.5% (10,422) ended without purchase and 1,908 (15.5%) with purchase.** 17 features, including bounce and exit rates, page values, visitor type, traffic type, browser/OS and weekend. These map directly onto TwinStore's traffic-source and device variables. Dataset DOI 10.24432/C5F88Q. The license field was not returned by the UCI API (UCI generally uses CC BY 4.0 **[not re-verified]**). — [UCI](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/468/online+shoppers+purchasing+intention+dataset); [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00521-018-3523-0)
- **Coveo SIGIR eCom 2021 Data Challenge** (Tagliabue, Greco, Roy, Yu, Chia, Bianchi, Cassani, 2021, arXiv:2104.09423): **more than 30M browsing events** (product detail, add to cart, purchase), plus search queries with clicked and unclicked items and catalog metadata (images). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09423)
- **Amazon-M2** (Jin, W., Mao, H., Li, Z., et al., NeurIPS 2023 D&B, KDD Cup 2023, arXiv:2307.09688): millions of sessions across six locales and languages. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.09688)
- **Expedia ranking field experiment** (Ursu, R. M., 2018, "The Power of Rankings", *Marketing Science* 37(4):530–552, doi:10.1287/mksc.2017.1072): randomized rankings. Rankings affect **what consumers search but, conditional on search, not purchases**. Average position effect is $1.92. Data are available through the journal. Wadi & Ma (2026) used it as the human benchmark (166,036 searches). — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2017.1072)
- **WebShop human demonstrations** (more than 1,600) and **Mind2Web crowdsourced action sequences** (more than 2,000 tasks) are human trajectories. They are instruction-following rather than naturally motivated shopping. — [WebShop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.01206); [Mind2Web](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.06070)
- **Twin-2K-500** (Toubia, Gui, Peng, Merlau, Li, Chen, 2025, arXiv:2505.17479; SSRN 5265253): N = 2,058 representative US participants, 4 waves, 500 questions, including behavioral-economics replications and a **pricing survey**. Wave 4 repeats earlier tasks to give a test–retest ceiling. HF `LLM-Digital-Twin/Twin-2K-500`, **cc-by-4.0**. · *Results*: human test–retest accuracy is **81.72%** across 17 tasks. GPT-4.1-mini twins reach **71.72% (87.67% of test–retest)**, against 59.17% for random guessing. The twins replicate framing, conjunction, WTA/WTP and others, but fail on sunk cost (the effect reverses), Allais and base rate. (From full text.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17479); [HF](https://huggingface.co/datasets/LLM-Digital-Twin/Twin-2K-500)
- **Psych-101** (Binz et al., *Nature* 644:1002–1009, 2025, doi:10.1038/s41586-025-09215-4): trial-by-trial data from **more than 60,000 participants, over 10M choices, 160 experiments**. Used to train Centaur, which predicts held-out participants better than cognitive models. — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09215-4); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.20268)
- **Persona corpora** (synthetic, not ground truth): Tianyi-Lab/Personas, about 1M LLM-generated personas (apache-2.0), from Li et al. 2025; PersonaHub, 1B personas (Ge et al. 2024, arXiv:2406.20094; HF cc-by-nc-sa-4.0). — [HF Personas](https://huggingface.co/datasets/Tianyi-Lab/Personas); [arXiv PersonaHub](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.20094)

### Inferences
- A practical three-tier validation for TwinStore:
  1. **Step level** on OPeRA. Re-render OPeRA observations as TwinStore-style numbered options and compute next-action accuracy, action-type F1 and outcome F1 against the 8–22% prompt-only baselines.
  2. **Session/funnel level**: compare bounce, conversion, pages per session and add-to-cart → purchase ratios against UCI Online Shoppers (15.5% conversion), Coveo and ShopCART, by traffic source and device.
  3. **Position level**: test rank-inspection slopes against Ursu/Expedia, following Wadi & Ma.
- OPeRA's persona fields (age, gender, income, shopping habits) overlap with TwinStore's persona schema. That enables a persona-shuffle ablation like Customer-R1's (39.58% → 28.94%).

### Gaps
- No public dataset found pairs real web-shopping sessions with **post-visit satisfaction, SEQ or NPS** from the same users. Exit-survey validation therefore has to rely on published usability norms (e.g., Sauro & Dumas 2009 for SEQ, Section 6) or on new human data collection.
- No public human A/B-test corpus for e-commerce UI changes with known effect sizes was found. SimUSER's 55 food-platform A/B tests are proprietary.

## 3. Recommender-system and conversational user simulators

### Takeaway
LLM recommender simulators (RecAgent, Agent4Rec, SimUSER, ContextSim) are mostly validated by discrimination tasks: can the agent tell items its real counterpart consumed from distractors (about 65–80% accuracy at a 1:1 ratio). Rating error and rating-distribution plots, LLM-judged "believability" and, in the best cases, correlation with real A/B tests are also used. Critiques show data leakage and flattened, overly positive behavior.

### Cited Findings
- **Bougie, N., Marconi, G. M., Ye, X., Watanabe, N. (2026). Beyond Offline A/B Testing: Context-Aware Agent Simulation for Recommender System Evaluation (ContextSim). arXiv:2604.09549.** Validated through "offline A/B testing correlation". Parameters tuned in the simulator improved real-world engagement. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09549)
- **Bougie, N. & Watanabe, N. (2025). SimUSER: Simulating User Behavior with LLMs for Recommender System Evaluation. arXiv:2504.12722.** · *Human data*: MovieLens, AmazonBook, Steam, plus **55 proprietary online A/B tests** on a food recommendation platform. · *Metrics*: accuracy, precision, recall and F1 for discriminating interacted items; rating RMSE and MAE; **Spearman correlation between simulated and real A/B outcomes** (values shown only in a figure, p < .05); believability scored by **GPT-4o, not humans**. · *Results*: MovieLens 1:1 accuracy is 0.79 for SimUSER, 0.69 for Agent4Rec and 0.58 for RecAgent. Rating RMSE is 0.50 vs 0.76 for Agent4Rec. In live deployment, view ratio went from 0.521 to 0.561. (From full text.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12722)
- **Balog, K. & Zhai, C. (2025). User Simulation in the Era of Generative AI: User Modeling, Synthetic Data Generation, and System Evaluation. arXiv:2501.04410.** A cross-disciplinary synthesis of user simulation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04410)
- **Wang, L., Zhang, J., Yang, H., Chen, Z., Tang, J., Zhang, Z., Chen, X., et al. (2025). User Behavior Simulation with Large Language Model-based Agents (RecAgent). ACM TOIS, doi:10.1145/3708985; arXiv:2306.02552.** Claims simulated behaviors are "very close" to real humans. Also simulates information cocoons and conformity. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.02552); [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1145/3708985)

## 4. Synthetic consumers and LLM market research

### Takeaway
Marketing research finds that LLM "synthetic consumers" often get **directions and averages right**: WTP close to conjoint estimates, purchase-intent rankings at 80–90% of test–retest ceilings, about 78% sign accuracy for field experiments. They get **distributions, heterogeneity and some causal effects wrong**. Direct numeric Likert elicitation produces unrealistic distributions. Confounding in simulated experiments needs explicit design.

### Cited Findings
- **Tsai, Y.-L. & Lai, Y.-H. (2026). Seeing Is Not Perceiving: When Synthetic Consumers Can and Cannot Pretest Visual Marketing. arXiv:2609.25677** (six preregistered studies). GPT-4o-mini and GPT-5.4-mini were tested on six canonical visual-marketing experiments. **No configuration reproduced more than two of the six human effects**, and one reversed. In-context evidence steers means, but responses show **less than half the natural human spread**. The authors propose a Calibrate–Intervene–Deploy protocol. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25677)
- **Maier, B. F., Aslak, U., Fiaschi, L., Rismal, N., Fletcher, K., Luhmann, C. C., Dow, R., Pappas, K., et al. (2025). ### Inferences
- Maier et al. are directly relevant to TwinStore's exit survey. **Asking a model for a satisfaction, SEQ or NPS number directly is known to produce collapsed distributions.** TwinStore should report KS similarity and correlation attainment against a human reference, or consider SSR-style elicitation.

## 5. General human-behavior simulation fidelity and known failure modes

### Takeaway
The general literature establishes that LLMs can match **aggregate patterns and treatment-effect directions** surprisingly well. Examples: r = .85 for survey-experiment effects; 83–86% of test–retest for interview-grounded agents; "algorithmic fidelity" of subgroup distributions. The same literature documents recurring failure modes: low variance and homogeneity, caricature and flattening of groups, ordering and label bias, prompt and temporal instability, hyper-accuracy and over-rationality, and response biases unlike humans'.

- **Ashokkumar, A., Hewitt, L., Ghezae, I., Willer, R. (2026). ## 6. Position and option-order bias in LLM/VLM product choice, and the visual grounding of numbered options

### Takeaway
LLM choice among listed options is strongly affected by option IDs and positions, and the size and *direction* of the effect differ by model and version. Humans also show rank effects, so the right validation target is **human-like position sensitivity**, not zero sensitivity. Here human-likeness means a monotone decline with rank, mainly at the search and inspection stage, as in Ursu 2018. LLMs instead show "lost-in-the-middle" curves.

### Cited Findings
- **Wadi & Ma (2026)**: humans show a monotone rank penalty on inspection (−0.0019 per rank) and almost none on choice. LLMs show smaller inspection penalties but U-shaped "lost-in-the-middle" curves, depending on reasoning effort. (Section 1.) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22697)
- **Allouah et al. (2025, ACES)**: position coefficients vary in sign across providers and versions and persist in text-only "headless" mode. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02630)
- **Bansal et al. (2025, Magentic Marketplace)**: a first-proposal bias gives a 10–30× speed advantage. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25779)
- **Zheng, C., Zhou, H., Meng, F., Zhou, J., Huang, M. (2024). Large Language Models Are Not Robust Multiple Choice Selectors. ICLR 2024 (Spotlight). arXiv:2309.03882.** "Selection bias" comes mostly from **token bias toward specific option IDs**. Their PriDe method estimates a prior by permuting options and then debiases. 20 LLMs tested. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03882)
- **Pezeshkpour, P. & Hruschka, E. (2023). LLMs Sensitivity to the Order of Options in Multiple-Choice Questions. arXiv:2308.11483** (Findings of NAACL 2024 **[venue not re-verified]**). Reordering options changes performance by **13% to 75%**. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11483)
- **Hou, Y., Zhang, J., Lin, Z., Lu, H., Xie, R., McAuley, J., Zhao, W. X. (2024). LLMs are Zero-Shot Rankers for Recommender Systems. ECIR 2024. arXiv:2305.08845.** LLM rankings are **biased by item position and popularity in the prompt**; bootstrapping and prompting can mitigate this. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08845)
- **Kumar, A. & Lakkaraju, H. (2024). Manipulating LLMs to Increase Product Visibility. arXiv:2404.07981.** A strategic text sequence on a product page raises its chance of being the top recommendation. Relevant to the robustness of TwinStore's A/B comparisons when page copy changes. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.07981)
- **Ursu (2018), Marketing Science.** Human field-experiment benchmark for rank effects. Rankings affect search, not purchase conditional on search. — [Crossref](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2017.1072)
- **Zheng, B., Gou, B., Kil, J., Sun, H., Su, Y. (2024). GPT-4V(ision) is a Generalist Web Agent, if Grounded (SeeAct). arXiv:2401.01614** (ICML 2024 **[venue not re-verified]**). Visual web agent on Mind2Web, with 51.1% of tasks done on live sites under oracle grounding. **Grounding** is the bottleneck. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01614)
- **Yang, J., Zhang, H., Li, F., Zou, X., Li, C., Gao, J. (2023). Set-of-Mark Prompting Unleashes Extraordinary Visual Grounding in GPT-4V. arXiv:2310.11441.** Overlaying numbered marks on image regions improves grounding. This is the methodological precedent for TwinStore's numbered on-screen options. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11441)

### Inferences
- TwinStore's shuffled option numbers are a recognized debiasing approach (cf. permutation in PriDe and randomized ordering in Dominguez-Olmedo). They only address ID and label bias, though. **Spatial position bias** in a screenshot (top-left vs bottom-right) remains and should be measured separately with randomized layouts (ACES-style conditional logit). The result should be compared with human rank curves (Ursu; Wadi & Ma) to show that the remaining position sensitivity is human-like in shape.

### Gaps
- No study was found that compares **VLM screenshot-based** position effects with human eye-tracking or click heatmaps on the same e-commerce page.

## 7. Validation methodology: what reviewers will expect

### Takeaway
Classic simulation-validation frameworks have three main elements. Sargent separates conceptual, operational and data validity. Grimm's pattern-oriented modeling requires matching multiple patterns at different scales. Fagiolo et al. distinguish calibration from validation and favor stylized-fact replication. Recent critiques say generative ABMs mostly rely on "believability" and rarely show operational validity. A defensible TwinStore paper should therefore report several independent fidelity checks at the step, session, population and treatment-effect levels. These should use held-out human data, be benchmarked against human test–retest or natural variation, and use explicit distribution metrics.

### Cited Findings
- **Larooij, M. & Törnberg, P. (2025). Do LLMs Solve the Problems of Agent-Based Modeling? A Critical Review of Generative Social Simulations. arXiv:2504.03274.** "Validation remains poorly addressed, with many studies relying solely on subjective assessments of model 'believability'." Even the most rigorous studies fail to show **operational validity**. LLMs may make ABM problems worse. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.03274)
- **Anthis, J. R., Liu, R., Richardson, S. M., Kozlowski, A. C., Koch, B., Evans, J., Brynjolfsson, E., Bernstein, M. (2025). LLM Social Simulations Are a Promising Research Method. ICML 2025 (position). arXiv:2504.02234.** Five tractable challenges. Simulations can "already be used for pilot and exploratory studies". The paper calls for iterative evaluation. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.02234)

### Inferences — metrics catalogue drawn from the papers above
- **Step level:** next-action exact match, action-type macro and weighted F1, click-type F1, session-outcome (buy vs leave) F1 (Lu 2025; OPeRA; Customer-R1; Shop-R1).
- **Session and funnel level:** per-session action counts vs humans (AgentA/B Table 1); conversion-rate alignment (SimPersona, 78%); search depth and share of single-inspection sessions (Wadi & Ma); outside-option and no-purchase rate (Wadi & Ma; Chen 2026).
- **Distribution level:** KS similarity (Maier); marginal, structural and individual fidelity (Choi 2026); variance ratio vs humans (Bisbee; Tsai & Lai). KL, JS and Wasserstein distances are rarely reported numerically in this literature, so reporting them for action-type, session-length and price-paid distributions is a differentiator.
- **Benchmarked to human noise ceilings:** normalized accuracy relative to test–retest (Park 2024: 83–86%; Twin-2K-500: 87.67%); correlation attainment (Maier: 90%); simulation error vs natural human drift (TwinStore physical: 0.017 vs 0.012).
- **Treatment-effect level:** sign agreement rate (Chen et al. 2025: 78%; AgentA/B direction); correlation of predicted and actual effect sizes (Ashokkumar/Hewitt: r = .85); Spearman correlation across many real A/B tests (SimUSER, 55 tests).
- **Discrimination and Turing tests:** distribution-based Turing test (Mei et al. 2024); Turing Experiments (Aher et al. 2023). LLM-judged believability (SimUSER) is weaker evidence, per Larooij & Törnberg.
- **Robustness:** multiple models and versions (ACES drift); prompt variants (Bisbee); persona shuffle and ablation (Customer-R1; OPeRA); option permutation (Zheng; Dominguez-Olmedo); and a strict calibration/validation split (Fagiolo), i.e., do not tune prompts on the same human data used to claim fidelity. Maier note that their reference statements were tuned on the same 57 surveys.

- No evaluation framework specific to **VLM web-shopper simulators** was found. Balog & Zhai (2025) and Gao et al. (2023) are general.
- Venue information for several arXiv items (UXAgent, SeeAct, Santurkar, Tjuatja, Pezeshkpour, Chen et al. PNAS, Park–Schoenegger–Zhu) is from memory and marked **[not re-verified]**.
