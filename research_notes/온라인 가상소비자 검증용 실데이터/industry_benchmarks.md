# Published industry benchmarks for real online-store behavior (aggregate KPIs) to compare against TwinStore's simulated online shoppers

Research date: 2026-10-25. Most "live" benchmark pages (Littledata, IRP Commerce, Statista, Dynamic Yield) are rolling and change month to month. Each value below is recorded as it appeared when accessed in October 2026, with its stated window. Reliability tags used throughout:
- **[PRIMARY]**: read on the publisher's own page.
- **[PRIMARY-PR]**: the publisher's press release or marketing page. The full report is gated.
- **[SECONDARY]**: a third-party re-quote. The original page was not reached.
- **[SNIPPET]**: seen only in a search-result summary, not on a fetched page. Treat as unverified.

No Korea-specific figures appeared inside any of the global reports reached.

## 1. Conversion rate benchmarks: overall, by device, industry, region and traffic source

### Takeaway
Session-level ecommerce conversion clusters at **about 1.3–2.0% of sessions/visits** in the broad panels:
- Statista: 1.3% (Q2 2026).
- Littledata (Shopify): median 1.4%, mean 1.9%.
- IRP Commerce (UK/IE): 1.97%.

Enterprise/personalization-vendor panels report higher figures: Dynamic Yield 2.66% (secondary), and Contentsquare retail desktop 3.7%. Desktop out-converts mobile in Contentsquare's data (+74% across all industries, 40–70% across retail sub-industries). The Littledata Shopify panel is the exception: mobile and desktop medians are almost equal there. Returning visitors convert at about 1.7x the rate of new visitors (Contentsquare: 2.9% vs 1.7%).

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **Choose the comparison by panel type.**
  - For a broad mid-market web shop (the closest analogue to a rendered TwinStore shop), the defensible "real-world" band is **~1.3–2.0% session conversion**, with the Littledata IQR roughly 0.5%–2.7% (bottom 20% to top 20%).
  - Contentsquare's retail desktop 3.7% / mobile ~2% and Dynamic Yield's 2.66% reflect larger, enterprise-optimized sites.
- **Industry ordering is consistent across publishers.** Beauty, food/grocery and pet convert high; home/furniture and luxury convert low:
  - Littledata: Beauty 2.8% > Health 2.5% > Fashion 1.3% > Home 0.9%.
  - Dynamic Yield: Pet 5.7%, Beauty 5.32%, F&B 4.58% > Fashion 2.77% > Home 1.29% > Luxury 0.63%.
  - Contentsquare: grocery desktop 11.4% vs luxury desktop 1.4%.

  This rank ordering is a good qualitative validation target even where levels differ.
- **The device gap depends on the panel.** It is large in the Contentsquare enterprise panel (desktop +40–74%) but nearly absent in Littledata's Shopify panel (1.5% desktop vs 1.6% mobile medians). A simulator showing desktop > mobile is consistent with Contentsquare. It should not be presented as universal.

### Gaps
- Contentsquare's absolute all-industry conversion rate and per-industry levels sit in the gated report / 50+ metric explorer and were not reachable.
- Several secondary sites quote numbers for Contentsquare's 2025 retail edition (desktop 3.81%, mobile 2.03%, mobile 75.9% of retail traffic) and Q4 2025 all-industry figures (2.3% or 2.5%). [SNIPPET; unverified]
- Dynamic Yield device conversion rates conflict across re-quoters [SNIPPET; unverified]:
  - mobile 2.86% vs desktop 2.23%, Oct 2025 – Sep 2026;
  - alternatively mobile 2.88%, tablet 2.85%, desktop 2.37%;
  - desktop reported as falling to 1.84% in Sep 2026.

  The primary page could not be fetched (503 / redirect).
- Salesforce Shopping Index: no primary Q2 2026 figures were reached. A Salesforce blog snippet mentions Spain Q2: 73% mobile traffic; footwear converting at 2.24%, health & beauty at 2.29%. [SNIPPET]
- Monetate Ecommerce Quarterly, Similarweb, Unbounce, Oberlo, Growcode and Invesp: not reached in this pass. Smart Insights' compilation returned HTTP 403.
- No regional (APAC/EU country) conversion table from a primary global source was obtained. Exceptions: Statista's Switzerland figure, and Barilliance country cart-abandonment (Section 3).

## 2. Funnel step rates: product view → add-to-cart → checkout → purchase

### Takeaway
The best primary, percentile-level funnel data is Littledata's Shopify panel (median shop, GA4 sessions):
- **6.8% of sessions add to cart.**
- **~51–52% of checkout-starting sessions complete.**
- **1.4% of sessions purchase.**

Its cart abandonment median is **78–79%**, which equals 1 − 1.4/6.8 and so is internally consistent. No reliable primary benchmark for the "product-page-view rate" (share of sessions viewing a PDP) was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Littledata add-to-cart rate** (share of sessions with an add-to-cart). Sample: 433 Shopify stores, Jul 20 – Oct 17, 2026. [PRIMARY]

  | Statistic | Add-to-cart rate |
  |---|---|
  | Median | **6.8%** |
  | Bottom 10% | 2.1% |
  | Bottom 20% | 3.4% |
  | Top 20% | 12% |
  | Top 10% | 17% |

  Source: [Littledata benchmark hub](https://www.littledata.io/average)
- **Littledata add-to-cart by device and industry.** Sample: 424 stores, 90 days to Oct 10, 2026; overall median 6.7%. [SECONDARY quoting Littledata]
  - Mobile median **5.4%** (top 10% 12%); desktop median **9.7%** (top 10% 35%).
  - Industry medians:

    | Industry | Median | Stores |
    |---|---|---|
    | Beauty & Skincare | 13% | 38 |
    | Food & Beverage | 11% | 30 |
    | Health & Supplements | 7.6% | 34 |
    | Fashion & Apparel | 6.3% | 75 |
    | Home & Furniture | 4.7% | 61 |

  - Stores qualify only with at least 1,000 sessions and 90 purchases in the window, and add-to-cart tracked at least as often as purchases.
  - Source: [Propel Commerce re-quoting Littledata](https://propelcommerce.io/blog/average-add-to-cart-rate-shopify-2026)
- **Littledata checkout completion rate** (checkout-starting sessions that purchase). [PRIMARY]
  - Median **51%**; bottom 10% 17%; bottom 20% 27%; top 20% 67%; top 10% 76%. — [Littledata benchmark hub](https://www.littledata.io/average)
  - The 7 Oct 2026 blog (426 stores) gives median **52%**, bottom 20% 30%, top 20% ≥68%. — [Littledata blog](https://www.littledata.io/blog/2026/10/07/shopify-benchmarks-2026-what-they-mean)
- **Older Littledata panel.** 2,800 stores, 2023 vintage, now retired: add-to-cart **4.6%** of sessions, about 45% of checkouts completed, 1.4% of sessions ordering. [SECONDARY] — [Propel Commerce](https://propelcommerce.io/blog/average-add-to-cart-rate-shopify-2026)
- **Small-sample full funnel.** 21 Shopify stores, Q2 2026: sessions → ATC 5.95%; reached checkout 4.46% of sessions; completed purchase 2.16% of sessions. [SNIPPET; small sample] — [DTC Pages](https://www.dtcpages.com/blog/ecommerce-conversion-rate-benchmarks-2026)
- **Constructor (searchers vs non-searchers, by vertical).** Add-to-cart rate for searchers vs non-searchers: Health & beauty 49% vs 23%; Apparel 37% vs 22%; General merchandise 30% vs 21%; Home 30% vs 11%; Specialty & hobby 25% vs 12%. These are likely per visitor, not per session; the denominator is not stated. Sample: 113 retail sites, Oct–Dec 2024. [PRIMARY-PR] — [Constructor press release via AI Journal](https://aijourn.com/?p=283778)
- **Contentsquare.** AI-referred journeys reach purchase in about 2 fewer pages than other traffic. [PRIMARY-PR] — [Contentsquare retail – conversions](https://contentsquare.com/guides/retail-digital-experience/conversions/)

### Inferences
- Implied median-shop funnel, from the Littledata medians (an approximation, since medians are not strictly multiplicative):

  | Stage | Share of sessions |
  |---|---|
  | Visit | 100% |
  | Add to cart | ~6.8% |
  | Reach checkout | ~2.7% (1.4% ÷ 0.51) |
  | Purchase | ~1.4% |

  This implies **cart → checkout ≈ 40%** and **checkout → purchase ≈ 51%**. These are reasonable target ratios for TwinStore's visit→product_view→add_to_cart→checkout→payment→purchase funnel.
- Desktop ATC (9.7%) exceeds mobile ATC (5.4%) while mobile and desktop *conversion* medians are similar (Littledata). So mobile carts convert relatively better in that panel, which matters if the simulator applies a uniform device penalty.

### Gaps
- No primary benchmark found for the **product-view rate** (share of sessions with ≥1 PDP view), or for a separate **payment-step** completion rate (checkout → payment info → order).
- Dynamic Yield add-to-cart figures are [SNIPPET only] and conflict, so they are unusable without the primary page:
  - global 5.96%;
  - Dec 2025: F&B 10.09%, Beauty 8.48%, apparel 6.33–7.12%;
  - device: 6.17% desktop / 6.07% mobile / 6.47% tablet;
  - two different 2024 device tables in Smart Insights.
- Monetate funnel benchmarks were not reached.

## 3. Cart abandonment and checkout abandonment

### Takeaway
- The canonical figure is **Baymard's 70.22%**: mean of 50 studies, updated Sep 22, 2025.
- Session-tracking vendors measure higher:
  - Littledata Shopify median: 78%.
  - Dynamic Yield trailing 12 months: ~77.4–77.8% (secondary).
  - Barilliance 2019: 77.13%.
- Every source puts **mobile highest, desktop lowest**: roughly 79–81% vs 69–74% in recent data.

### Cited Findings
- **Baymard Institute.** [PRIMARY]
  - Average documented cart abandonment: **70.22%**, from **50 studies**, last updated **Sep 22, 2025**. — [Baymard cart abandonment list](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)
  - Recent studies in the meta-list:

    | Study | Rate |
    |---|---|
    | Uptain 2025 | 71.72% |
    | SaleCycle 2023 | 79.53% |
    | Fresh Relevance 2022 | 68.70% |
    | Kibo 2021 | 79.30% |
    | Fresh Relevance 2021 | 59.22% |
    | SaleCycle 2021 | 81.08% |
    | Fresh Relevance 2020 | 57.60% |
    | SaleCycle 2020 | 84.27% |
    | Barilliance 2019 | 77.13% |

    Source: [Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)
  - Abandonment reasons (US shoppers, Baymard survey): 42% abandoned because "just browsing / not ready to buy". Among the rest:
    - extra costs too high: 40%
    - delivery too slow: 20%
    - didn't trust site with card: 19%
    - forced account creation: 18%
    - checkout too long or complicated: 17%
    - website errors: 17%
    - returns policy: 13%
    - total cost not visible up front: 12%
    - card declined: 10%
    - not enough payment methods: 9%

    Source: [Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate). Conflict: Shopify's re-quote of Baymard 2025 lists 39% extra costs, 21% slow delivery, 19% account, 19% trust and 18% complicated checkout ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ecommerce-conversion-rate)), and another re-quote says 43% "just browsing" ([Zipchat](https://www.zipchat.ai/blog/cart-abandonment-benchmarks-and-causes)). These likely reflect different survey waves.
  - Checkout form length: the average US checkout has **23.48 form elements (14.88 form fields)**; the ideal is 12–14 elements (7–8 fields). Across 60 leading sites benchmarked, the average is 39 potential checkout improvements per site. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)
- **Littledata cart abandonment** (Shopify, GA4; 433 stores; Jul–Oct 2026). Median **78%**; best 10% 56%; best 20% 65%; worst 20% 89%; worst 10% 94%. [PRIMARY] — [Littledata hub](https://www.littledata.io/average). The 7 Oct 2026 blog (426 stores) gives median **79%**. — [Littledata blog](https://www.littledata.io/blog/2026/10/07/shopify-benchmarks-2026-what-they-mean)
- **Dynamic Yield** (customers' stores, "past twelve months"). [SECONDARY]
  - Overall **77.38%**: mobile **79.53%**, tablet **72.92%**, desktop **69.56%**.
  - Beauty & personal care is highest at 79.94%; pet care & vet is lowest at 50.4%.
  - Original page: marketing.dynamicyield.com/benchmarks/cart-abandonment-rate/ (unreachable).
  - Source: [CareCart re-quote](https://carecart.io/shopify-cart-abandonment-rate-2026/)
  - A different re-quote gives 77.81% (rolling 12 months, mid-2026, "200M+ monthly users"), mobile 80.45%, desktop 68.62%. — [Zipchat re-quote](https://www.zipchat.ai/blog/cart-abandonment-benchmarks-and-causes)
- **Barilliance** (own customer data; sample sizes not stated). [PRIMARY, older]
  - 2019: global 77.13% (the device section says 77.73%); phone 80.79%, desktop 73.93%.
  - 2015: mobile 85.65%, tablet 80.74%, desktop 73.07%.
  - 2019 by country: Spain 86.15% (highest); United States 71.86%; Netherlands 65.49% (lowest).
  - Black Friday 2019: 73.78%; Cyber Monday 2019: 68.94%.
  - Source: [Barilliance cart abandonment statistics](https://www.barilliance.com/cart-abandonment-rate-statistics/)
- **Barilliance Black Friday–Cyber Monday 2016** (50M users tracked, Nov 25–28, 2016). Desktop 63.8%, tablet 64.5%, mobile 78.1%; average 68.8%. [PRIMARY, old] — [Barilliance BF 2016](https://www.barilliance.com/shopping-cart-abandonment-rates-on-black-friday-weekend/)

### Inferences
- For comparison with a simulator that computes abandonment as 1 − orders/carts at session level, the **Littledata (78–79%)** and **Dynamic Yield (~77%)** session-tracking figures are the like-for-like targets. Baymard's 70.22% is a cross-study mean with heterogeneous definitions.
- Note the coincidences: Dynamic Yield's "mobile 79.53%" exactly matches SaleCycle 2023's 79.53%, and Uptain 2025's 71.72% matches the commonly misquoted "SaleCycle mobile 71.72%". At least one re-quote may be a misattribution, so cite the Baymard meta-list for those values.
- The widely re-quoted "SaleCycle: mobile 71.72% / desktop 28.28%" sums to 100%. It is a share of abandonments by device, **not** an abandonment rate, and should not be used as one.

### Gaps
- Checkout abandonment specifically (starting checkout without ordering) is available only as Littledata's inverse checkout completion: median 48–49% abandonment of started checkouts.
- No primary 2025–2026 SaleCycle report was reached. Industry-level abandonment from SaleCycle (home 74–79%, fashion 71–76%, luxury ~80%) is [SECONDARY, undated].

## 4. Bounce rate, pages per session and session duration

### Takeaway
- GA4-era bounce benchmarks for ecommerce sit at about **40–42%**: Littledata Shopify median 40%; IRP Commerce 41.55%.
- Pages per session median is **2.7** in the Littledata Shopify panel. Contentsquare reports much deeper grocery journeys (9.8 desktop / 4.8 mobile page views).
- Current, citable primary **session-duration** benchmarks were not found; the only figure is a 2020 secondary (2m 57s).

### Cited Findings
- **Littledata bounce rate.** Definition (GA4): share of sessions that were *not engaged*, i.e. under 10 s, one page and no conversion. Median **40%**; best 10% 9.3%; best 20% 19%; worst 20% 55%; worst 10% 63%. Sample: 433 Shopify stores, Jul–Oct 2026. [PRIMARY] — [Littledata hub](https://www.littledata.io/average)
- **Littledata pages per session.** Median **2.7**; bottom 10% 1.4; bottom 20% 1.7; top 20% 4.1; top 10% 5.3. [PRIMARY] — [Littledata hub](https://www.littledata.io/average)
- **IRP Commerce bounce rate** (UK/IE). **41.55% (Sep 2026)** vs 40.14% (Sep 2025). [PRIMARY] — [IRP Commerce](https://www.irpcommerce.com/en/gb/ecommercemarketdata.aspx)
- **Contentsquare retail (2025 data), page views per session.** Grocery 9.8 desktop / 4.8 mobile; Consumer Electronics 3.9 desktop / 4.3 mobile. [PRIMARY-PR] — [Contentsquare retail guide](https://contentsquare.com/guides/retail-digital-experience/)
- **Contentsquare 2026, all industries.** Engagement fell 10% YoY ("less time on site, fewer pages, less scrolling"). Bounce rate improved, especially for AI-influenced traffic (−5%, relative). Frequent users of the tooling bounce at 45.4% vs 49.2% for others; this is a customer-subset statistic from a search summary. [PRIMARY-PR / SNIPPET] — [Contentsquare bounce-rate blog](https://contentsquare.com/blog/bounce-rate-benchmarks/); [Contentsquare 2026 report page](https://contentsquare.com/blog/digital-experience-benchmark-report)
- **Contentsquare 2025.** Among paid-social users, bounce rate rose 9.2% and page views fell 8.7%. On slow-loading pages, 53% of users left after one page. [PRIMARY-PR] — [Contentsquare 2025 press](https://contentsquare.com/press/2025-digital-experience-benchmarks/)
- **Contentsquare 2024.** The typical desktop session had **1.5 more pages** than the typical mobile session. [SNIPPET] — [Contentsquare 2024 Explorer](https://csquad.contentsquare.com/benchmark24-explorer)
- **Contentsquare 2020.** Apparel had the lowest bounce rate of all verticals (42%) and grocery the highest (52%). [SNIPPET of PRIMARY PDF] — [Contentsquare 2020 Benchmark PDF](https://go.contentsquare.com/hubfs/eBooks/2020%20Digital%20Experience%20Benchmark/2020%20Digital%20Experience%20Benchmark%20Report%20PDF%20English.pdf)
- **Wolfgang Digital KPI Report 2020.** Average 5 pages per session, 2m 57s average session duration, 41% bounce rate. [SECONDARY] — [StoreGrowers re-quote](https://www.storegrowers.com/ecommerce-benchmark-metrics/)
- **Wolfgang 2017.** The strongest correlation in the study was between session duration and conversion rate (0.60). — [Wolfgang 2017 KPI PDF](https://www.wolfgangdigital.com/uploads/case-studies/Wolfgang_Digital_2017_E-commerce_KPI_Benchmarks_Study.pdf) (via search snippet)

### Inferences
- GA4 "bounce" means non-engaged session (<10 s, one page, no conversion). Universal Analytics bounce meant a single-page session, and Contentsquare's definition is not stated. TwinStore should compute its bounce with the GA4 definition when comparing with Littledata or IRP 2025–2026 figures.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) primary session-duration benchmark by device or industry was reachable. Contentsquare's "time spent" values are behind the gated explorer.
- Databox and GA benchmarking (Google's own) were not reached.

## 5. Site search: usage share, conversion of searchers, zero-result rate

### Takeaway
The strongest primary data is Constructor's 2025 study (113 retail sites, Oct–Dec 2024):
- **24% of visitors search**;
- searchers produce **44% of revenue** and **42% of conversions**;
- searchers convert at **2.5x** the rate of non-searchers.

## 12. How publishers define the metrics (comparability notes)

### Takeaway
The definitions differ enough to move the numbers by a factor of two or more:
- session-based vs visitor-based conversion;
- GA4 "not-engaged" bounce vs single-page bounce;
- carts-vs-orders vs survey-based abandonment;
- per-display vs per-interaction popup conversion.

TwinStore should report the matching definition for each benchmark it is compared against.

### Cited Findings
- **IRP Commerce:**
  - Conversion = Transactions ÷ Sessions × 100.
  - AOV = Revenue ÷ Orders.
  - CPA = Marketing Cost ÷ Revenue × 100.
  - Channel share = Channel Revenue ÷ Total Revenue × 100.
  - Last-click attribution; free traffic is grouped under "Direct".
  - Source: [IRP Commerce](https://www.irpcommerce.com/en/gb/ecommercemarketdata.aspx)
- **Littledata:**
  - Conversion = sessions with ≥1 purchase ÷ all GA4 sessions, per store, then percentiles across stores. The median is preferred over the mean.
  - Bounce = not-engaged sessions (<10 s, one page, no conversion).
  - Mobile share excludes tablets.
  - Revenue per session = purchase revenue ÷ all sessions.
  - Sources: [Littledata conversion](https://www.littledata.io/average/ecommerce-conversion-rate-(all-devices)); [Littledata hub](https://www.littledata.io/average); [Littledata RPS](https://www.littledata.io/benchmarks/revenue-per-session)
- **Statista** describes conversion as the share of visits to e-commerce sites ending in a purchase; no formal definition or underlying source is shown. — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/439576/global-e-commerce-conversion-rate/)
- **Contentsquare** does not define conversion, bounce or engagement on its open pages. Changes are generally relative YoY percentages, not percentage points. Its 2025–2026 editions compare Q4 vs Q4. — [Contentsquare 2026 report](https://contentsquare.com/blog/digital-experience-benchmark-report); [Contentsquare 2025 press](https://contentsquare.com/press/2025-digital-experience-benchmarks/)
- **Baymard's 70.22%** is a simple mean of 50 third-party studies from 2006–2025, each with its own methodology. — [Baymard](https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate)
- **Wisepops:** popup conversion = emails collected ÷ displays (email-signup popups only). — [Wisepops](https://wisepops.com/blog/popup-stats)
- **Omnisend:** open rate = opened ÷ delivered; conversion = conversions ÷ delivered; click-to-conversion = converters ÷ clickers. — [Omnisend](https://www.omnisend.com/blog/cart-abandonment-emails/)
- **Wolfgang 2014–2017 studies** averaged per website, not per visit; the 2014 study covered 30 retail and travel sites. [SNIPPET] — [Wolfgang 2016 PDF](https://www.wolfgangdigital.com/uploads/general/eComKPI2016-Public.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Recommended "anchor" benchmarks for the paper**, chosen as primary, recent, with sample and definition disclosed:
  - Littledata 2026 Shopify percentiles: CR, ATC, checkout completion, cart abandonment, bounce, pages/session, AOV, RPS, mobile share.
  - IRP Commerce Sep 2026: UK conversion and AOV by category; device and channel revenue share.
  - Baymard 70.22%: cart abandonment meta-average.
  - Contentsquare 2026: device gap, new vs returning, channel conversion, mobile traffic share.
  - Constructor 2025: site-search share and uplift.
  - Klaviyo/Omnisend: cart-recovery email.
  - Wisepops: popups.
  - Adobe 2025 holiday: mobile transaction share, AI-referral conversion.
- Comparing simulator output against **percentile bands** (e.g., Littledata's 20th–80th) rather than a single mean is more defensible, since real stores vary by roughly 5x within a single platform.

### Gaps
- Contentsquare's and Dynamic Yield's metric definitions could not be confirmed from primary pages: Contentsquare's are gated, and Dynamic Yield's pages are offline or redirected to Mastercard.
