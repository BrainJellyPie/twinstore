TwinStore v0.6.0 makes online stores something you can see and shape: design a
shop by asking for it, give it one of eleven looks, watch every shopper move
through it, and read a report built around them.

- **Design with AI.** Tell the designer what you want — "make it feel
  premium", "remove the pressure tactics", "make buying as quick as possible" —
  and the text model changes the store's design options within the designer's
  own limits. Each change shows as a chip you can undo, a preview switches
  between the saved store and the changes on desktop or phone, and **Test
  against the store** saves the result as a variant and opens a run comparing
  the two on the same shoppers.
- **Eleven page styles:** classic, minimal, luxury, brutalist, playful,
  marketplace, editorial, neon (dark), retro web, organic and tech — colours,
  corners, serif or monospace headings, header, menu row, banner and product
  cards change while every option stays drawn and numbered. The designer shows
  your own store in every style, nineteen templates start in a fitting one, and
  quick experiments try a luxury or minimal look. Vision-model shoppers see the
  look; the classic style draws pages exactly as before.
- **A report built around shoppers.** A dark stage opens each run with its
  verdict (a clear winner, a control that holds, or too close to call) and
  every store's conversion as a glowing ring. The **shopper stream** animates
  every virtual shopper entering each store at the same moment and stopping at
  the funnel stage where the visit ended — hover to find the same person in the
  other stores, click to watch them. A **page flow** chart shows the first six
  pages of every visit and how it ended, with the most common route. Shopper
  cards with filters, per-measure tiles, interval charts, outcome bars and a
  segment heat map replace plain tables; section links stay in reach and a
  full-width mode hides the store list.
- **Watch any visit.** Play a shopper's visit as an animation — each page
  loads, the view scrolls to their pick, the cursor clicks and their thought
  appears — or two stores side by side with the step where they part ways, and
  download it as a GIF.
- **A rival shop.** Shoppers who leave without buying may try another store
  that sells what they want; each store sees how many went, what they bought
  there and the revenue it lost.
- **The marketing studio meets both twins.** Each variant of an A/B test can
  become a variant of an online store (its prices, discounts, category prices
  and packages applied to the products with the same SKU; shelf signs and
  moves are listed as not carried over), and **Compare online** opens a run of
  the two; a price test's best price goes online the same way. **Keep it in
  the store** reviews a tested variant on its layout and saves it as a new
  layout. A store shopper's page shows the same customer's online visits, one
  click from their replay, and "make the web shop look premium" in the command
  bar opens Design with AI with that request.
- **Find shoppers faster.** Online stores open from the home screen and every
  store card; in a physical store's results a **Track a shopper** banner, a
  Follow in 3D button on every listed shopper and a large one on each shopper's
  page put the 3D camera on them.
- **Dependable results.** Welcome popups promise only their configured
  discount, product pictures are frozen with each run, rival visits get their
  own random draws and their real traffic source in the clickstream CSV,
  animated visits recover from failed images, GIF export uses less memory, and
  a phone banner's button no longer covers its headline. Elsewhere: drafted
  event hours, free prices and report commentary are validated and kept,
  echoed interview answers are refused, local models select starter products
  with fewer retries, and concurrent layout and product saves no longer
  recreate deleted work.

The interface is available in all six languages. Rule shoppers follow
illustrative, uncalibrated probabilities and do not react to a page's look; use
vision-model shoppers to test designs, and read differences between variants
rather than absolute conversion rates.
